
## One set of statements, Six Different Readers

- Equity Investor - Buy, hold or sell?
- Banker/Creditor - Will they repay?
- Regulator - Solvent and compliant?
- Non-Profit Board - Financially sustainable?
- Church/Foundation Treasurer - Faithful stewardship
- Government Auditor - Public money as reported?

## Six step framework
This will be used through out the analysis
1. Strategy
2. Accounting
3. Financial
4. Forecasting
5. Valuation
6. Decision

**1-3 is more on the company = Backward/Inward**
Understanding the company as it is

**4-6 Forward looking =**  
Using that understanding to project ahead and act

*We cannot skip the steps.*

## Business Strategy Analysis
*What is the source of this company's competitive advantage - and is it sustainable?*

- Identify the company's primary profit drivers and industry economics.
- Assess whether its strategy is cost leadership, differentiation, or an unclear mix
- Evaluate the biggest business risks it faces.

## Accounting Analysis
*Do the numbers reflect economic reality - or has accounting choice distorted the picture?*

- Evaluate how much flexibility the company had in its accounting choices
- Identify areas prone to distortion: revenue recognition, reserves, related parties.
- Adjust statements where necessary for genuine comparability

## Financial Analysis
*How profitable, efficient, and financially healthy is this company?*

- Use ration analysis to evaluate profitability, liquidity, solvency, efficiency
- Use cashflow analysis

## Forecasting
*What is this company likely to look like financially in two to five years?*

- Translate Steps 1-3 into explicit, defensible assumptions.
- Build linked pro forma income statement, balance sheet, and cashflow

## Valuation
*What is the company actually worth - and how does that compare to its price?*

- Convert the forecast into an estimate of instrinsic value.
- Triangulate: discounted cashflow, residual income, market multiples.
- Understand why methods disagree - the gap is often more informative than any single number.

## Decision
*Given everything above, what should I - or the stakeholder I'm analyzing for - actually do?*

- Translate valuation and analysis into a concrete recommendation
- Tie the recommendation explicitly to a specific user
